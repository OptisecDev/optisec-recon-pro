How the Service enforces scan authorization

  • Passive checks — dns, whois, osint, ssl and headers — may be run against any target. They generate the same kind of traffic as an ordinary visitor or public lookup.
  • Active checks — nmap, ports, subdomain, xss, sqli, ssrf, lfi and redirect — run only when the target is:
    • a domain you have verified on your account with a DNS TXT record carrying your verification token (covers the domain and all its subdomains); or
    • one of the public, intentionally vulnerable training targets listed in the Service, and then only for the checks that site's own published terms allow (for example, only nmap and ports against scanme.nmap.org).
  • IP addresses cannot be verified, so active checks against a bare IP address are refused unless it is a listed training target.
  • Verified domains are re-checked before use once the last check is older than the configured period (7 days by default); if the record or file is gone, the verification is revoked.
  • A scan's active traffic is confined to the authorized host(s); connections elsewhere (for example through redirects) are blocked. An unauthorized target is limited to passive checks.
  • Each scan records the basis on which it was allowed.

Verification is not permission. Verifying a domain shows you control its DNS or web content; it does not prove you are legally allowed to test it, or the infrastructure behind it. Passive checks being technically available against any target does not make every use of them lawful. You must not attempt to bypass, disable or work around these controls.
